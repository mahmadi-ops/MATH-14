    Sections: Green’s Theorem in the Plane, Surface Integrals: Flux Through a Surface, and The Divergence Theorem.

    Work each problem yourself first; the full solution follows immediately below it. The same sixteen problems, with the numbers randomised and every answer checked automatically, are in Review Problems #4—Practice.

    Problem 6.2.1. Outward Flux Through the Surface of a Box.

    Evaluate the outward flux of the vector field
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} \mathbf{F} = 3x^2\,\mathbf{i} + z^4\,\mathbf{j} + e^z\,\mathbf{k} \end{equation*}
    through the surface \(S\) of the solid box \(W\) given by \(0 \le x \le 2\text{,}\) \(0 \le y \le 3\text{,}\) \(0 \le z \le 1\text{.}\) Set up the triple integral that the Divergence Theorem provides, and evaluate it.
    🔗
    🔗

    Problem 6.2.2. The Box With the Largest Flux.

    Let \(\nabla \cdot \mathbf{F} = 4\left(4 - x\right)\) and \(0 \le a, b, c \le 12\text{.}\)
    🔗
    (a) Find the flux of \(\mathbf{F}\) out of the rectangular solid \(0 \le x \le a\text{,}\) \(0 \le y \le b\text{,}\) \(0 \le z \le c\text{,}\) as an expression in \(a\text{,}\) \(b\) and \(c\text{.}\)
    🔗
    (b) For which values of \(a\text{,}\) \(b\) and \(c\) is that flux largest, and what is the largest flux?
    🔗
    🔗

    Problem 6.2.3. Verifying the Divergence Theorem on a Cylinder.

    Verify the Divergence Theorem for the vector field
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} \mathbf{F} = \langle 3x,\ 4z,\ 5y \rangle \end{equation*}
    and the region \(x^2 + y^2 \le 1\text{,}\) \(0 \le z \le 2\text{:}\) compute the flux \(\iint_S \mathbf{F}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{n}}\,d\sigma\) across the closed surface directly, compute \(\iiint_W \nabla \cdot \mathbf{F}\,dV\) over the solid, and check that the two agree.
    🔗
    🔗

    Problem 6.2.4. Flux Through a Sphere.

    Use the Divergence Theorem to evaluate the surface integral \(\displaystyle\iint_{S} \mathbf{F}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{n}}\,d\sigma\text{,}\) where
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} \mathbf{F} = \langle x^3,\ 4,\ z^3 \rangle \end{equation*}
    and \(S\) is the sphere \(x^2 + y^2 + z^2 = 9\text{,}\) oriented outward.

    Problem 6.2.5. Flux Through a Paraboloid Cap.

    Let \(\mathbf{F}(x,y,z) = z\tan\!\left(y^2\right)\mathbf{i} + z^3\ln\!\left(x^2+1\right)\mathbf{j} + 3z\,\mathbf{k}\text{.}\) Use the Divergence Theorem to find the flux of \(\mathbf{F}\) across the part of the paraboloid \(x^2 + y^2 + z = 5\) that lies above the plane \(z = 1\) and is oriented upward.

    Problem 6.2.6. Radial Field Through a Closed Cylinder.

    Problem 6.2.7. Flux Through a Helicoid.

    🔗

    oriented upward.

    Problem 6.2.11. Three Gradient Fields and the Unit Circle.

    (a) Each of the vector fields
    🔗
    \begin{align*} \mathbf{F} \amp = 2y\,\mathbf{i} + 2x\,\mathbf{j},\\ \mathbf{G} \amp = \frac{3y}{x^2+y^2}\,\mathbf{i} + \frac{-3x}{x^2+y^2}\,\mathbf{j},\\ \mathbf{H} \amp = \frac{4x}{\sqrt{x^2+y^2}}\,\mathbf{i} + \frac{4y}{\sqrt{x^2+y^2}}\,\mathbf{j} \end{align*}
    is a gradient vector field on some domain (not necessarily the whole plane). Find potential functions for \(\mathbf{F}\) and \(\mathbf{H}\text{,}\) choosing the constant so that the potential is \(0\) at the origin, and find the constant \(C\) for which \(g(x,y) = C\arctan\!\left(\dfrac{x}{y}\right)\) is a potential function for \(\mathbf{G}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    (b) Find the line integrals of \(\mathbf{F}\text{,}\) \(\mathbf{G}\) and \(\mathbf{H}\) around the curve \(C\text{,}\) the unit circle in the \(xy\)-plane centered at the origin and traversed counterclockwise.
    🔗
    (c) For which of the three vector fields can Green’s Theorem be used to calculate the line integral in part (b), and why?
    🔗
    🔗

    Problem 6.2.14. Divergence of a Family of Radial Fields.

    Let \(r = \left(x^2+y^2\right)^{1/2}\) and consider the vector field \(\mathbf{F} = r^A\!\left(x\,\mathbf{i} + y\,\mathbf{j}\right)\text{,}\) where \(r \ne 0\) and \(A\) is a constant. Note that \(\mathbf{F}\) has no \(z\)-component and is independent of \(z\text{.}\)
    🔗
    (a) Find \(\nabla \cdot \left(r^A\!\left(x\,\mathbf{i} + y\,\mathbf{j}\right)\right)\text{,}\) and show that it can be written in the form \(\nabla \cdot \mathbf{F} = c\,r^{a}\) for constants \(c\) and \(a\) depending on \(A\text{.}\)
    🔗
    (b) For what values of \(A\) is \(\nabla \cdot \mathbf{F}\) positive, negative, or zero?
    🔗
    (c) What is the sign of the flux out of a small sphere centered at \((1,1,1)\) when \(A = 2\text{,}\) and when \(A = -5\text{?}\) How would your answers change for a small sphere centered at the origin?
    🔗
    🔗
