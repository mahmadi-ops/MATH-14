Example 4.9. Work done by the gravitational field along a helix.

Calculate the line integral
\begin{equation*} \int_C \frac{-x}{\left(x^2+y^2+z^2\right)^{3/2}}\,dx +\frac{-y}{\left(x^2+y^2+z^2\right)^{3/2}}\,dy +\frac{-z}{\left(x^2+y^2+z^2\right)^{3/2}}\,dz \end{equation*}
over the curve \(\mathbf{r}(t)=\langle\cos t,\ \sin t,\ t\rangle\) from \((1,0,0)\) to \((1,0,2\pi)\text{.}\)
🔗
Solution.
This integral is in the differential form \(\int_C M\,dx+N\,dy+P\,dz\) with \(\mathbf{F}=\langle M,N,P\rangle\text{,}\) and we recognize the field:
\begin{equation*} \mathbf{F} =\frac{\langle -x,\,-y,\,-z\rangle}{\left(x^2+y^2+z^2\right)^{3/2}} =-\frac{\mathbf{r}}{\left|\mathbf{r}\right|^3}, \end{equation*}
the gravitational field of Figure 4.3. The curve is a helix that climbs one full turn, from \((1,0,0)\) at \(t=0\) to \((1,0,2\pi)\) at \(t=2\pi\text{;}\) see Figure 4.10.
🔗
We write the field along the curve in terms of \(t\text{.}\) On the helix,
\begin{equation*} x^2+y^2+z^2=\cos^2 t+\sin^2 t+t^2=1+t^2, \end{equation*}
so
\begin{equation*} \mathbf{F}\bigl(\mathbf{r}(t)\bigr) =\frac{1}{\left(1+t^2\right)^{3/2}} \langle -\cos t,\ -\sin t,\ -t\rangle. \end{equation*}
The velocity vector is \(\mathbf{v}=\langle -\sin t,\ \cos t,\ 1\rangle\text{,}\) and the dot product collapses nicely:
\begin{equation*} \mathbf{F}\cdot\mathbf{v} =\frac{\cos t\sin t-\sin t\cos t-t}{\left(1+t^2\right)^{3/2}} =\frac{-t}{\left(1+t^2\right)^{3/2}}. \end{equation*}
🔗
Therefore, using the substitution \(u=1+t^2\text{,}\) \(\tfrac{du}{2}=t\,dt\text{,}\)
🔗
\begin{align*} \int_C M\,dx+N\,dy+P\,dz \amp = \int_{t=0}^{t=2\pi}\frac{-t}{\left(1+t^2\right)^{3/2}}\,dt = -\frac{1}{2}\int_{u=1}^{u=1+4\pi^2} u^{-3/2}\,du\\ \amp = \left(-\frac{1}{2}\right)(-2)\, \frac{1}{\sqrt{u}}\Bigg|_{u=1}^{u=1+4\pi^2} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{1+4\pi^2}}-1\approx -0.84. \end{align*}
The work is negative, and the picture explains why: the gravitational field always points toward the origin, while the helix climbs steadily away from it, so the tangential component of the force opposes the motion at every instant. The accumulation of this negative work is animated in Figure 4.11.
