Exercise 2. Which Shapes Can Carry This Density?

With \(\delta(x,y) = x^2-y^2\) and the shapes of Figure 5.4.2: type the numbers of the two shapes that can carry \(\delta\) as a density, in increasing order and separated by a comma (for instance 1,3).

Solution.
A density cannot be negative, so we need \(x^2 - y^2 \ge 0\text{,}\) that is \(|x| \ge |y|\text{.}\) That holds on shapes \(2\) and \(4\text{,}\) and fails on shapes \(1\) and \(3\text{.}\)
🔗
Shape \(2\) is the triangle of area \(r^2/2\) and shape \(4\) the sector of area \(\pi r^2/8\text{.}\) The triangle contains the sector’s range of radii and has the larger area, so \(\iint_{R_2}\delta\,dA \ge \iint_{R_4}\delta\,dA\text{:}\) shape \(2\) is heavier.
