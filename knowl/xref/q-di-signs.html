Checkpoint 2.1.13. Signs, without computing.

Assume that \(R\) is the square of (2.1.3) and choose all the correct statements below. Remember to justify your answers. Note that Figure 2.1.14 and Figure 2.1.15 are helpful in answering this question.
🔗
  1. \(\displaystyle \displaystyle\iint_R x\,dA \gt 0\)
    🔗
    🔗
  2. \(\displaystyle \displaystyle\iint_R x\,dA = 0\)
    🔗
    🔗
  3. \(\displaystyle \displaystyle\iint_R x\,dA \lt 0\)
    🔗
    🔗
  4. \(\displaystyle \displaystyle\iint_R (y-x)\,dA = 0\)
    🔗
    🔗
  5. \(\displaystyle \displaystyle\iint_R (y-x)\,dA \lt 0\)
    🔗
    🔗
  6. \(\displaystyle \displaystyle\iint_R (y-x)\,dA \gt 0\)
    🔗
    🔗
Answer.
B and D are the correct statements.
🔗
For B: the plane \(z=x\) of Figure 2.1.14 lies above the \(xy\)-plane on the half of \(R\) where \(x \gt 0\) and the same distance below it on the half where \(x \lt 0\text{,}\) because \(R\) is symmetric about the line \(x=0\text{.}\) The two signed volumes cancel, so \(\iint_R x\,dA = 0\text{.}\) In symbols, replacing \(x\) by \(-x\) carries \(R\) to itself and changes the sign of the integrand.
🔗
For D: the same argument applies to \(y\text{,}\) so \(\iint_R y\,dA = 0\) as well, and therefore
🔗
\begin{equation*} \iint_R (y-x)\,dA = \iint_R y\,dA - \iint_R x\,dA = 0 - 0 = 0 . \end{equation*}
Figure 2.1.15 shows this directly: the plane \(z=y-x\) cuts \(R\) along the diagonal \(y=x\text{,}\) and the wedge above that diagonal is congruent to the wedge below it. Statements A, C, E and F all claim a nonzero sign, so all four are false.
