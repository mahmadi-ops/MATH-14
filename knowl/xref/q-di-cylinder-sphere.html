Checkpoint 2.2.13. A ball cut by a cylinder.

Set up a double integral for computing the volume of the region bounded by the cylinder \(x^2+(y-1)^2=1\) and the sphere \(x^2+y^2+z^2=1\text{.}\) See Figure 2.2.14.
🔗
Answer.
The solid is the part of the ball that lies inside the cylinder. It is bounded below by \(z=-\sqrt{1-x^2-y^2}\) and above by \(z=\sqrt{1-x^2-y^2}\text{,}\) so its height over a point of the region of integration is \(2\sqrt{1-x^2-y^2}\text{.}\)
🔗
The region of integration \(D\) is the shadow of the solid: the points lying inside both circles \(x^2+y^2=1\) and \(x^2+(y-1)^2=1\text{.}\) Those circles cross where \(\sqrt{1-x^2}=1-\sqrt{1-x^2}\text{,}\) that is at \(x=\pm\frac{\sqrt3}{2}\text{,}\) \(y=\frac12\text{.}\) Between those values of \(x\text{,}\) the lower boundary of \(D\) is the cylinder and the upper boundary is the sphere, so
🔗
\begin{equation*} V = \iint_D 2\sqrt{1-x^2-y^2}\,dA = \int_{-\sqrt3/2}^{\sqrt3/2}\!\! \int_{1-\sqrt{1-x^2}}^{\sqrt{1-x^2}} 2\sqrt{1-x^2-y^2}\;dy\,dx . \end{equation*}
This is a region of the kind this section is about: \(D\) is bounded above and below by the graphs of functions of \(x\text{,}\) which is Subsection 2.2.1. In polar coordinates the same integral becomes far friendlier still.
