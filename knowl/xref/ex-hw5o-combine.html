Exercise 2. Three Integrals Into One.

Combine the three integrals of Exercise 2 into a single polar integral. In \(\displaystyle\int\!\!\int r^3\sin\theta\cos\theta\, dr\,d\theta\text{,}\) the radius runs from to and \(\theta\) from to .
🔗
Solution.
The three pieces together are the part of the annulus \(1 \le r \le 2\) lying between the \(x\)-axis and the line \(y = x\text{.}\) Since \(xy = r^2\sin\theta\cos\theta\) and \(dA = r\,dr\,d\theta\) by (2.4.1),
🔗
\begin{equation*} \int_0^{\pi/4}\!\!\int_1^2 r^3 \sin\theta\cos\theta \,dr\,d\theta . \end{equation*}
