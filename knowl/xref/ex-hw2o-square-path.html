Exercise 3. Around the Unit Square.

Evaluate \(\displaystyle\int_C \frac{1}{x^2+y^2+1}\,ds\) over the boundary \(C\) of the unit square of Figure 5.2.1.
🔗
The side from \((0,0)\) to \((1,0)\) contributes ; the side from \((1,0)\) to \((1,1)\) contributes ; and the whole integral equals .
🔗
Solution.
On the side from \((0,0)\) to \((1,0)\text{,}\) \(f = 1/(t^2+1)\) and \(ds = dt\text{,}\) giving \(\tan^{-1}(1) = \pi/4 \approx 0.785\text{;}\) the side on the \(y\)-axis gives the same. On each of the other two sides \(f = 1/(t^2+2)\text{,}\) giving \(\frac{1}{\sqrt2}\tan^{-1}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt2}\right) \approx 0.615\text{.}\) Adding,
🔗
\begin{equation*} \int_C f\,ds = \frac{\pi}{2} + \sqrt2 \tan^{-1}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt2}\right) \approx 2.441 . \end{equation*}
A line integral of a scalar function does not depend on the direction of travel, and opposite sides of the square give equal contributions.
