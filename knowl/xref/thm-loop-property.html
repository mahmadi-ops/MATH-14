Theorem 1.5.17. Loop Property of Conservative Fields.

The following statements are equivalent.
🔗
  1. \(\oint_C \mathbf F\cdot d\mathbf r = 0\) around every loop (that is, closed curve \(C\)) in \(D\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
  2. The field \(\mathbf F\) is conservative on \(D\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
🔗
🔗

Proof.

First, we prove that (2) implies (1); we assume that \(\mathbf F\) is conservative. Then, as shown in Figure 1.5.18, we can think about any loop as \(C = C_1 \cup C_2\text{,}\) where \(C_1\) goes from \(A\) to \(B\) and \(C_2\) returns from \(B\) to \(A\text{.}\) Reversing the orientation of \(C_2\) produces the curve \(-C_2\text{,}\) and \(C_1\) and \(-C_2\) both connect \(A\) to \(B\text{.}\) Since \(\mathbf F\) is conservative, path independence gives \(\int_{C_1} \mathbf F\cdot d\mathbf r = \int_{-C_2} \mathbf F\cdot d\mathbf r\text{,}\) and therefore
🔗
\begin{align*} \oint_C \mathbf F\cdot d\mathbf r \amp= \int_{C_1} \mathbf F\cdot d\mathbf r + \int_{C_2} \mathbf F\cdot d\mathbf r\\ \amp= \int_{-C_2} \mathbf F\cdot d\mathbf r + \int_{C_2} \mathbf F\cdot d\mathbf r\\ \amp= -\int_{C_2} \mathbf F\cdot d\mathbf r + \int_{C_2} \mathbf F\cdot d\mathbf r\\ \amp= 0. \end{align*}
Hence \(\oint_C \mathbf F\cdot d\mathbf r = 0\) for any loop \(C\text{.}\)
🔗
Next, we prove that (1) implies (2); we assume that \(\oint_C \mathbf F\cdot d\mathbf r = 0\) around every loop in \(D\text{.}\) Let \(A\) and \(B\) be any two points of \(D\text{,}\) and let \(C_1\) and \(C_2\) be any two paths in \(D\) from \(A\) to \(B\text{.}\) Travelling along \(C_1\) from \(A\) to \(B\) and then back along \(-C_2\) from \(B\) to \(A\) traces a loop \(C = C_1 \cup (-C_2)\) in \(D\text{,}\) so the assumption applies to it:
🔗
\begin{align*} 0 \amp= \oint_C \mathbf F\cdot d\mathbf r\\ \amp= \int_{C_1} \mathbf F\cdot d\mathbf r + \int_{-C_2} \mathbf F\cdot d\mathbf r\\ \amp= \int_{C_1} \mathbf F\cdot d\mathbf r - \int_{C_2} \mathbf F\cdot d\mathbf r . \end{align*}
Therefore \(\int_{C_1} \mathbf F\cdot d\mathbf r = \int_{C_2} \mathbf F\cdot d\mathbf r\text{.}\) Since \(C_1\) and \(C_2\) were arbitrary paths joining \(A\) to \(B\text{,}\) and \(A\) and \(B\) were arbitrary points of \(D\text{,}\) the line integral of \(\mathbf F\) is path-independent in \(D\text{;}\) that is, \(\mathbf F\) is conservative on \(D\) (Definition 1.5.4).
