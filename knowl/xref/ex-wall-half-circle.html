Example 1.3.10. The wall on a half circle.

Calculate the line integral \(\displaystyle\int_C \left(2 + x^2 y\right) ds\text{,}\) where \(C\) is the upper half of the circle \(x^2+y^2=1\text{,}\) as shown in Figure 1.3.11.
🔗
Solution.
By (1.3.18), this line integral is the surface area of the wall of Figure 1.3.11, whose height is the integrand \(2 + x^2 y\) and whose base is the upper half of the circle \(x^2+y^2=1\text{.}\) We can parametrize \(C\) as \(x(t) = \cos t\text{,}\) \(y(t) = \sin t\text{,}\) \(0 \le t \le \pi\text{.}\) Hence
\begin{align*} ds \amp = \sqrt{\left(\frac{dx}{dt}\right)^2 + \left(\frac{dy}{dt}\right)^2}\,dt\\ \amp = \sqrt{\sin^2 t + \cos^2 t}\,dt = dt , \end{align*}
🔗
and therefore
\begin{align*} \int_C \left(2 + x^2 y\right) ds \amp = \int_0^{\pi} \left(2 + \cos^2 t\,\sin t\right) dt\\ \amp = \left(2t - \frac13\cos^3 t\right)\Big|_0^{\pi}\\ \amp = 2\pi + \frac23 . \end{align*}
