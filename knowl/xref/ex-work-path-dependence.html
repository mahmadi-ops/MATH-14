Example 1.4.13. The same endpoints, two paths, two answers.

Calculate the work done by the force
\begin{equation*} \mathbf{F}(x,y)=\left\langle y,\ 2x\right\rangle \end{equation*}
in moving a particle from \(O(0,0)\) to \(P(1,1)\) along each of the following two paths:
  • \(C_1\text{,}\) the straight segment from \(O\) to \(P\text{,}\) parametrized by \(\mathbf{r}_1(t)=\left\langle t,\ t\right\rangle\text{,}\) \(0\le t\le 1\text{;}\)
    🔗
    🔗
  • \(C_2\text{,}\) the arc of the parabola \(y=x^2\) from \(O\) to \(P\text{,}\) parametrized by \(\mathbf{r}_2(t)=\left\langle t,\ t^2\right\rangle\text{,}\) \(0\le t\le 1\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
🔗
Solution.
Both curves start at \(O(0,0)\) and end at \(P(1,1)\text{,}\) so any difference between the two answers can come only from the paths themselves. We use (1.4.9) for each one.
🔗
Along \(C_1\text{.}\) Here \(x=t\) and \(y=t\text{,}\) so \(\mathbf{F}=\left\langle y,\ 2x\right\rangle =\left\langle t,\ 2t\right\rangle\) and \(\mathbf{r}_1'(t)=\left\langle 1,\ 1\right\rangle\text{.}\) Therefore
🔗
\begin{align*} W_1 \amp = \int_{C_1}\mathbf{F}\cdot d\mathbf{r} = \int_0^1\left\langle t,\ 2t\right\rangle \cdot\left\langle 1,\ 1\right\rangle dt\\ \amp = \int_0^1 3t\,dt = \frac{3}{2}. \end{align*}
Along \(C_2\text{.}\) Here \(x=t\) and \(y=t^2\text{,}\) so \(\mathbf{F}=\left\langle y,\ 2x\right\rangle =\left\langle t^2,\ 2t\right\rangle\) and \(\mathbf{r}_2'(t)=\left\langle 1,\ 2t\right\rangle\text{.}\) Therefore
🔗
\begin{align*} W_2 \amp = \int_{C_2}\mathbf{F}\cdot d\mathbf{r} = \int_0^1\left\langle t^2,\ 2t\right\rangle \cdot\left\langle 1,\ 2t\right\rangle dt\\ \amp = \int_0^1\left(t^2+4t^2\right)dt = \int_0^1 5t^2\,dt = \frac{5}{3}. \end{align*}
Since \(W_1=\dfrac{3}{2}\) and \(W_2=\dfrac{5}{3}\) are not equal, the line integral of \(\mathbf{F}\) from \(O\) to \(P\) depends on the path taken, and not on the endpoints alone. Knowing where a curve starts and ends is therefore not enough, in general, to determine the work. Deciding which fields are the exception—those for which the endpoints are enough—is the question taken up in Section 1.5.
